# Large Molecule Druggability Guide

## What Is a Large Molecule?
Large molecule therapeutics include monoclonal antibodies (mAbs), bispecific antibodies, antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), Fc-fusion proteins, nanobodies, and other protein-based biologics typically >1000 Da.

## Ideal Large Molecule Targets

### Accessibility
- **Extracellular or cell-surface targets**: Antibodies cannot penetrate cell membranes; ideal targets are extracellular domains, secreted proteins, or surface receptors
- **Soluble proteins**: Cytokines, growth factors, circulating antigens — easily engaged by antibodies
- **Receptor ectodomains**: Accessible for antibody binding; blocking ligand binding or receptor dimerization

### Epitope Considerations
- Target must present an accessible epitope for antibody binding
- Functional blocking requires epitope overlapping with ligand binding site, active site, or oligomerization interface
- Steric constraints on dense cell surfaces can limit activity

### Expression Context
- **Tumor antigens for oncology**: High tumor vs. normal tissue differential expression; internalization needed for ADCs
- **Cytokines/chemokines in inflammation**: Neutralization by soluble antibody in circulation or tissue
- **Cell surface receptors**: Blocking, activating, or receptor downregulation via ADCC/CDC

## Format Selection Guide

| Target Type | Preferred Format | Rationale |
|------------|-----------------|-----------|
| Soluble cytokine | Neutralizing mAb or trap | Directly blocks circulating factor |
| Cell surface receptor (blocking) | Antagonist mAb | Blocks ligand binding or signaling |
| Cell surface (killing) | ADC or ADCC-optimized mAb | Recruits cytotoxic effector function |
| Two separate targets | Bispecific mAb | Redirects effectors or dual pathway block |
| Long half-life needed | Fc fusion | FcRn recycling extends half-life |
| CNS target | Nanobody or transport-facilitated | Small size aids limited BBB penetration |

## Intracellular Targets
Standard antibodies cannot reach intracellular targets. Alternatives:
- ADC payload reaches intracellular target after internalization
- Intrabodies (intracellular expression, not therapeutic yet)
- Degraders (proteolysis-targeting chimeras adapted for biologics)
- mRNA/gene therapy to modulate expression

## Pharmacokinetic Considerations
- Typical mAb half-life: 2–4 weeks (FcRn-mediated recycling)
- SC dosing achievable if dose ≤1–2 mL
- Tissue penetration is limited (tumor penetration particularly challenging for large mAbs)
- Target-mediated drug disposition (TMDD): high target expression can cause rapid drug clearance

## Competitive Format Considerations
- First-in-class antibody vs. me-better (differentiated epitope, format, or half-life)
- Biosimilar risk for mature targets (post-patent expiry)
- Subcutaneous vs. IV formulation can be a competitive differentiator
